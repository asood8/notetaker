# Cell Biology

## The Cell Membrane

The cell membrane is a phospholipid bilayer that separates the interior of the
cell from the outside environment. It is selectively permeable, meaning it
controls which substances enter and leave.

Fluid mosaic model: the description of the membrane as a fluid layer of lipids
with proteins embedded throughout and free to move laterally.

- Integral proteins are embedded within the bilayer and often span it completely.
- Peripheral proteins are attached to the surface of the membrane.
- Cholesterol is a stabilizing molecule that keeps the membrane fluid at low
  temperatures and rigid at high temperatures.

## Transport

Passive transport is movement of a substance across a membrane without any
input of energy. Active transport is movement that requires energy, usually
supplied by ATP.

Osmosis: the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane, from
an area of lower solute concentration to an area of higher solute concentration.

The sodium-potassium pump is a membrane protein that moves three sodium ions out
of the cell for every two potassium ions it brings in.

# Cellular Respiration

## Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the breakdown of one glucose molecule into two molecules of
pyruvate, taking place in the cytoplasm. It produces a net gain of two ATP and
two NADH, and it does not require oxygen.

## The Krebs Cycle

The Krebs cycle is a series of reactions in the mitochondrial matrix that
oxidizes acetyl-CoA to carbon dioxide. Each turn of the cycle produces three
NADH, one FADH2, and one ATP.
